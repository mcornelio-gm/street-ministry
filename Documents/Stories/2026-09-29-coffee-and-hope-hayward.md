# Morning Conversations at San Lorenzo Creek

Beneath the Foothill Boulevard overpass in Hayward, San Lorenzo Creek flows quietly between concrete embankments and thick willow trees. For nearly a dozen individuals, the dry gravel banks serve as a hidden neighborhood.

Every other Sunday at 7:30 AM, Team 4 carries portable folding tables, insulated cambros of hot pancake batter, and propane griddles down the access ramp.

What started out as a quick grab-and-go meal has turned into a weekly sanctuary. Four men who live along the bank—Greg, 'Spider', Tony, and Chris—pulled up milk crates and plastic coolers to sit together.

*"On the streets, everyone is on guard 24/7. You don't sleep with both eyes closed,"* Tony shared over a plate of hot syrup-drizzled pancakes. *"Sitting at this table with you all is the only hour of my week where I feel like a human being instead of a target."*

We read Psalm 23 together, talked through the realities of addiction recovery, and connected Chris with a case manager at the Hayward Navigation Center. When people ask why we go to the hard places, it's for mornings like this.
