Every week, the volunteers on the Newark route gather at the Jack in the Box in Newark to serve. On the surface, it looks like a simple staging spot--a familiar parking lot where bags are packed, prayers are shared, and the night's outreach begins. Yet from that unassuming starting point, the faithfulness of the team stretches far beyond the Tri-City area.

Street ministry is built on relationships that endure through every season, even when the people we serve find themselves miles away from home. That truth came to life recently when the Newark team received word from a longtime street friend from the Hayward area who had become stranded more than a thousand miles away in Casper, Wyoming.

Far from familiar faces, support systems, and the community he knew, our friend had no way to get back to the Bay Area on his own. When the Newark route team heard about his situation, they didn't hesitate. True to the heart of the ministry, they stepped in to bridge the distance between urgent practical need and the tangible love of Christ.

Thanks to the generosity of faithful donors who support the ministry's outreach, the team was able to purchase a Greyhound bus ticket to bring our friend home. What followed was a grueling 46-hour journey across the country--miles of open highway, station transfers, and quiet anticipation--carrying him out of isolation and back toward the Hayward community where people knew his name and cared about his well-being.

As the team awaited his return later that week, our friend's story stood as a powerful reminder of why we gather at that Jack in the Box week after week. Sometimes sharing the gospel looks like a warm meal or a conversation on a street corner; other times, it looks like pooling resources to make sure a friend stranded in Wyoming knows he has not been forgotten, and helping bring him safely home.
