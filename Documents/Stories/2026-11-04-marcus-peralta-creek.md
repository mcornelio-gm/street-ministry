# A Warm Cup of Coffee and a Broken Tent Zipper

The morning fog was still clinging to the Eucalyptus trees along the creek trail off Peralta Boulevard when our three-person team set out. It was barely 44 degrees, and you could see your breath with every step.

Tucked into a small clearing about fifty yards off the bike path was a weathered gray dome tent. As we approached, we called out as trained: *"Good morning! Resonate Church Street Ministry here with hot coffee and breakfast burritos. Anyone up for company?"*

A raspy voice answered from inside: *"I could really use that coffee, please."*

A man named Marcus emerged. He was wrapped in two damp fleece throws, shivering. The door zipper of his tent had completely failed three nights earlier during a sudden downpour, letting freezing wind and drizzle blow straight into his sleeping bag.

We handed him a steaming insulated mug of sweet black coffee and hot foil-wrapped breakfast burritos. While Marcus ate, our team member David pulled an 8x10 heavy brown vinyl tarp and a pack of bungees from our outreach cart. Together with Marcus, we rigged a windbreak vestibule over his doorway to keep out the drafting creek wind.

*"People walk past this trail all day on their morning runs and pretend they don't see me,"* Marcus told us, his eyes welling up. *"You guys didn't just look at me—you saw that I was freezing."*

We sat with Marcus for another twenty minutes, exchanging stories about his background in warehouse logistics and praying together for his upcoming appointment with the Fremont Housing Navigation Center. We left him with dry thermal socks, a clean fleece beanie, and a promise: we will be back next Saturday morning at 8:00 AM.
