# Rebuilding Trust Along the Decoto Rail Tracks

Ray parked his 1998 green Chevy van along the industrial spur off Decoto Road in Union City. For the first two months our outreach team walked that stretch, Ray kept his curtains drawn and his windows shut tight. When we knocked, he would curtly say, *"Not interested, go away."*

We never pushed. Each time, we simply said, *"No problem, sir. We're leaving a gallon of cold water and two sandwiches on your front bumper in case you're hungry later."*

Consistency melts skepticism. In August, Ray's alternator died, leaving him with no interior light and no way to charge his phone. When our Saturday volunteer team noticed his hood propped up, team member Carlos—an automotive mechanic by trade—asked if he could take a look.

Two hours later, with a donated replacement alternator and jumper cables, Ray's van purred to life.

*"Nobody does something for nothing,"* Ray said, staring at his grease-stained hands. *"Why did you guys help me?"*

*"Because God loves you, Ray, and we believe our neighbors in Union City deserve to be cared for,"* Carlos replied.

Today, Ray is one of our route ambassadors. When newcomers park on the industrial strip, Ray walks them over to our team trailer on Saturday mornings and introduces them himself.
