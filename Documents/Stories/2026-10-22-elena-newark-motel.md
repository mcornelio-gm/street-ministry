# Finding Stability: Elena’s Journey Through the Newark Motel Corridor

When people picture homelessness, they often think of tents on sidewalks. But along Cedar Boulevard and Newark Boulevard, dozens of families live in transient motels—paying astronomical weekly rates just to keep their children off the pavement.

Elena is a 34-year-old single mother of two (ages 6 and 9). After an unexpected medical emergency and lost wages, she lost her apartment lease in Union City. For four months, her entire world has been condensed into Room 114: two double beds, a mini-fridge, and a small microwave.

Every Tuesday evening, our Newark Motel Care team delivers fresh groceries, fresh fruit, milk, sandwich fixings, and kid-friendly snacks that don't require a stovetop to cook. 

*"The hardest part,"* Elena shared, *"is that when you're paying $650 every single week just to avoid sleeping in the sedan, you can never save the first-and-last month deposit to rent a normal apartment again. You're trapped in a cycle."*

Beyond food, our volunteers provided Elena's kids with school backpacks, art supplies, and quiet reading time outside in the courtyard while Elena had an hour to breathe and talk through county voucher programs with one of our volunteer social workers.

Last week, Elena received the news she had been praying for: her emergency housing voucher was matched to a two-bedroom apartment in Hayward. We have committed to helping her furnish her new home with donated beds, a dining table, and kitchen essentials.
