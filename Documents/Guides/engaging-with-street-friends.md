## Questions to Draw People Out

When working with people experiencing homelessness, the goal is the same as God's question to Adam: **not to gather information for yourself**, but to gently invite them out of hiding, shame, isolation, or despair. Your tone matters most--warm, patient, unhurried, and genuinely caring. Eye contact, a calm voice, and no pressure to answer immediately make the biggest difference.

Here are suggested questions, grouped by purpose:

### "Where are you?" -- Showing Presence & Safety

These let them know you see them as a person, not a problem.

- 🙂 "How's your day going so far?"
- 🙂 "What's been the hardest part of today?"
- 🙂 "Where have you been sleeping lately?" (said gently, not demandingly)
- 🙂 "Is it okay if I sit with you for a minute?"

### Understanding Their Story -- Inviting Them to Share
- 🙂 "What's your name? What do you like people to call you?"
- 🙂 "How did you end up out here?" (Leave space for silence)
- 🙂 "What was life like before things got tough?"
- 🙂 "Is there a story behind that scar / that backpack / that dog?" (if something stands out naturally)

### Emotional & Spiritual "Hiding" Questions

These mirror God drawing out shame or pain:

- 🙂 "What's weighing on you the most right now?"
- 🙂 "If you could change one thing about today, what would it be?"
- 🙂 "Have you ever felt like God (or life) was asking you 'Where are you?'"
- 🙂 "What do you wish people understood about your situation?"

### Needs & Practical Help -- Moving Toward Restoration
- 🙂 "What do you need most right now to make it through the next few days?"
- 🙂 "Is there something specific I can pray for you about?"
- 🙂 "Have you eaten today? Is there anything your body needs?"
- 🙂 "Who's one person who's helped you before? What did they do that mattered?"

### Hope & Future-Focused Questions

- 🙂"What's one thing that still gives you hope?"
- 🙂"If things got better, what would a good day look like for you?"
- 🙂"Is there a dream or goal you've had to put on hold?"

### Tips for Using These Questions Effectively

- ✅ **Ask one at a time.** Silence is powerful--don't rush to fill it.
- ✅ **Listen more than you speak.** Often the best follow-up is simply, "Tell me more about that."
- ✅ **Respect boundaries.** If they don't want to talk, say, "That's okay. I'm here if you ever do."
- ✅ **Follow up with action.** Questions open the door; practical help (food, socks, a ride to shelter, prayer, connection to services) shows you care.
- ✅ **Watch for trauma.** Many are hiding deep pain, addiction, abuse, or mental health struggles. Never push. Your consistent, non-judgmental presence over time is often what draws them out.

**The Heart Behind It**  
Just as God knew Adam's location but still asked the question, you already know many are struggling. Your questions say:  
"I see you. You're not invisible. You matter. There's a way forward."

People experiencing homelessness often feel forgotten by God and people. When you ask with the same tenderness God showed Adam, David, Peter, and others, you become a living reminder that they are still being pursued by love.
